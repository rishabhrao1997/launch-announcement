# PR/FAQ: Tiffin service for office workers in Bengaluru

*Working-backwards draft v1, 3 Nov 2026. Working name: **Dabba Drop**.*

> **Note on numbers.** Figures marked † are assumptions or desk estimates that have not been checked yet. Treat them as `[N]` until verified on the ground. Prices we set ourselves (₹149, the 9pm cutoff, the 12:45 drop) are design choices, not facts.

## Scoping first: who is this actually for?

"Office workers in Bengaluru" is too broad to write a press release for. A Koramangala startup with a free cafeteria, a bank branch in Jayanagar, and a 6,000-person tech park on Outer Ring Road have different lunch problems. This draft narrows it to:

- **Customer:** salaried tech-park employees, 23 to 32, who **live alone or in a PG/shared flat with no real kitchen** and are **back in office 2 to 4 days a week** under return-to-office mandates.
- **Place:** one tech park to start (Manyata Tech Park is the working pick; the real pick is whichever park's facility team says yes first).
- **Today's lunch:** delivery apps on office days, PG food or Maggi on home days. No subsidised cafeteria they actually like.

If that person doesn't exist in big enough numbers at one address, nothing below matters.

# PRESS RELEASE

## Dabba Drop brings a ₹149 home-style lunch to Manyata Tech Park, only on the days you're in office

**Pick your office days on Sunday night. Your tiffin is waiting at your gate at 12:45. Work from home and you pay nothing.**

**BENGALURU, 1 February 2027.** Dabba Drop today opened in Manyata Tech Park for people who live alone, eat out every office day, and are tired of paying ₹300 for a lunch that arrives at 2pm. Subscribers choose which days they'll be in office, and a freshly cooked veg or non-veg meal is dropped at a fixed pickup point in their building at 12:45, for ₹149 a meal, all-in.

**The problem.** Return-to-office has put lakhs† of young Bengaluru tech workers back at their desks three or four days a week, many of them living in PGs or shared flats where cooking a lunch to carry isn't realistic. So lunch becomes a daily app order: ₹250 to ₹350† with fees, 40 to 70 minutes† at peak, food that is mostly oily and the same few cloud-kitchen brands, and a walk to the security gate to collect it. Over a month that is ₹5,000 to ₹7,000† on lunches nobody looks forward to. Traditional tiffin services are cheaper but are built for families: fixed monthly plans that charge for the days you work from home, delivery to a home address and not a tech-park tower, and no way to change plans when your manager moves the team day.

**The solution.** Dabba Drop is a tiffin built around a hybrid week. On Sunday night you tick the days you'll be in office; you can add or skip a day until 9pm the night before. Every meal for your building is cooked in one licensed kitchen that morning, packed, and delivered together in one run to one pickup point, so there is no rider hunting for your tower and no surge at 1pm. You walk down at 12:45, show your name on WhatsApp, and take your dabba. Two choices a day (one South Indian, one North Indian thali), a menu that doesn't repeat for four weeks, and non-veg on Wednesdays and Fridays. You only pay for days you eat.

My PG gives the same sambar every day, I'm not packing that. Now I tick three days on Sunday, go down to Block D at quarter to one, and it's 149. It tastes like someone actually cooked it."

# FAQ

## External (customer) questions

**1. How does it work, step by step?**
- Sunday by 9pm: pick your office days for the week on WhatsApp.
- Any day: add or skip tomorrow until 9pm tonight.
- 12:45 on your day: walk to your building's pickup point, give your name, take your dabba.
- Pay per meal from a UPI-topped wallet. No monthly lock-in.

**2. What does it cost?**
₹149 per meal including GST and delivery. First three meals ₹99. No subscription fee, no charge for days you skip. A 3-day-a-week user spends about ₹1,800 a month vs ₹3,000 to ₹4,200† on delivery apps for the same days.

**3. What if I forget to skip and work from home?**
If you skip after 9pm, you're charged, because the meal is already cooked. Two "oops" skips a month are free (refunded as credit). After that it's paid. We'll send a reminder at 8pm.

**4. What's on the menu?**
Two options daily: a South Indian meal (rice, sambar/rasam, poriyal, curd) and a North Indian thali (2 rotis, dal, sabzi, rice). Non-veg option on Wednesdays and Fridays. Four-week rotating menu, published every Sunday. No Jain or diet plans at launch.

**5. Why can't you deliver to my desk?**
Most tech parks don't let delivery staff past the lobby. One fixed pickup point per building is also what keeps the price at ₹149: one trip for 40+ meals instead of 40 trips.

**6. What if the food is late, cold, or bad?**
If it isn't at the pickup point by 12:55, that meal is free. If you don't like it, tap "bad meal" and we credit it, no questions, up to twice a month.

**7. What if I get sick from the food?**
Every meal comes from one FSSAI-licensed kitchen. Each day's batch is labelled and a sample is held for 48 hours. Report it on WhatsApp and you're refunded immediately while we investigate; we cover medical costs if the batch is at fault.

**8. Steel dabba or disposable?**
Sealed, compostable containers at launch. Steel dabbas need a daily return loop we can't run reliably yet.

**9. How is this different from Swiggy, Zomato, my cafeteria, or the tiffin aunty near my PG?**
- vs delivery apps: about half the price, no surge, same time every day, home-style food. Less choice.
- vs Zomato Everyday-type ₹89 meals†: we're more expensive. We're betting on a fixed time and place inside your building and a consistent menu. If you're happy with those, stay with them.
- vs your cafeteria: if yours is good and subsidised, you don't need us.
- vs a local tiffin service: they deliver to homes on monthly plans; we deliver to your office building and only charge for office days.

**10. Can I cancel?**
Anytime. Unused wallet balance is refunded to UPI within 3 working days.

**11. Dinner? Weekends? Other tech parks?**
Not at launch. One meal, one park, weekdays only, until that works.

## Internal (stakeholder) questions

**12. How many of these customers are there at one tech park, really?**
Unknown, and this is the first thing to find out. Manyata is said to house 1 lakh+† employees. We need about **600 active subscribers** (see Q15) at roughly 12 meals each per month. That is under 1% of the park, but the real filter is narrow: lives alone, no good cafeteria in their building, in office 2+ days, willing to walk to a pickup point. We don't know that share. A 2-week pre-order test answers it before we spend on a kitchen.

**13. Swiggy Daily shut down in 2020. Why would this work?**
Swiggy Daily† delivered subscription meals to homes across a city: low route density, a rider per few meals, and then COVID took demand away. The only thing that makes a ₹149 tiffin work is **density at one address**: 40 to 100 meals in one drop. If we can't get that density, we fail for the same reason they did. That is why we start with one park and refuse to expand until drops average 40+ meals.

**14. What if Zomato or Swiggy already sells ₹89 to ₹119 home-style meals at Manyata?**
Then we probably lose on price, and this question alone could kill the idea. Before launch, someone orders their meals for a week at the target park and checks price, time and quality. Our only defensible edges are a fixed in-building pickup (they can't easily batch to one point) and a predictable menu. If their product is good and live there, **pick a different park or kill it**. We do not try to out-discount a platform.

**15. What are the unit economics?**
Per meal at ₹149, all figures† estimates:

| Line | Pilot (partner kitchen) | Own kitchen (later) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue after 5% GST | ₹142 | ₹142 |
| Food, cooked and packed | ₹85 (we pay a partner per meal) | ₹55 |
| Packaging | ₹12 | ₹12 |
| Batched delivery (₹600 run ÷ 40 meals) | ₹15 | ₹15 |
| Payment fees | ₹3 | ₹3 |
| Waste, free meals, refunds (~5%) | ₹7 | ₹7 |
| **Contribution per meal** | **₹20 (~14%)** | **₹50 (~35%)** |

- Pilot is thin on purpose: it tests demand, not margins.
- Own kitchen with fixed costs of ~₹3.5 lakh/month† breaks even at ~7,000 meals/month, i.e. **~350 meals a day** or **~600 active subscribers**.
- Delivery cost is the swing factor. At 15 meals per drop, delivery is ₹40 a meal and contribution goes negative.

**16. Will the tech park even let us in?**
This is the biggest dependency and it isn't in our control. Large parks have facility managers, food-court operators with exclusive deals, and security rules about vendors. Options, in order:
1. Formal pickup point approved by facility management (best, slowest).
2. Pickup just outside the gate, like delivery apps do today (works, worse experience).
3. Partner with one company's admin team to use their floor or pantry (smaller, more stable).
If we can't get at least option 2 working cleanly at a park, we don't launch there.

**17. Own kitchen or partner kitchen?**
Partner first. A licensed caterer or cloud kitchen within 5 km cooks to our menu and we pay per meal. No lease, no staff, no equipment. We only build our own kitchen once a park sustains 250+ meals/day for 8 weeks.

**18. What happens the day 30 people get food poisoning?**
It would end the business: one kitchen, one batch, one office floor, and it spreads on Slack in an hour. Mitigation: FSSAI-licensed partner only, kitchen audit before signing, batch labels and retained samples, cooked-to-pickup under 3 hours, product liability insurance from day one. There is no mitigation that makes this risk zero. It is a cost of being in food.

**19. Hybrid work makes demand lumpy. Mondays and Fridays will be dead.**
Yes. Tuesday to Thursday may be 2 to 3x Monday and Friday†. The 9pm cutoff means we cook to orders, not forecasts, so waste stays low. The cost is idle kitchen capacity on low days, which is another reason to use a per-meal partner kitchen at first.

**20. Won't people get bored and churn in a month?**
Likely. Tiffin fatigue is the usual killer. A four-week rotation and two cuisines help, but the honest test is retention: what share of people who ate in week 1 still eat in week 6. If it's under 40%, the product doesn't work no matter how many sign up.

**21. Why not sell to employers instead (B2B meal benefit)?**
It's a better business if it works: one contract, guaranteed volume. But it means long sales cycles, procurement, competing with established corporate caterers†, and our customer becomes an admin team, not the person eating. Start B2C to prove people want the food, then use that data to pitch companies in the same park.

**22. What's the tech or AI angle here?**
Almost none, and that's fine. A WhatsApp bot, a wallet, and a sheet that sends the kitchen tomorrow's count by 9:15pm will run the pilot. Demand forecasting and route batching matter only at multiple parks. The hard parts are kitchen quality, on-time drops, and park access: operations, not software.

**23. Is this something we should be doing?**
This is the uncomfortable one. A tiffin service runs every working day at 12:45. Someone has to be reachable at 7am when the cook doesn't show up and at 12:50 when the drop is late. It cannot be a side project next to a full-time job. The founder's edge (building automations) covers maybe 10% of what makes this succeed. If there's no co-founder who wants to run food operations daily, the honest options are: run only the 2-week test to learn, or don't start.

**24. What do we need for the pilot, and what does it cost?**
- People: 1 ops owner (daily), 1 part-time builder for the WhatsApp ordering flow, 1 delivery runner.
- Partner: 1 FSSAI-licensed kitchen within 5 km of the park.
- Time: 3 weeks setup (kitchen, pickup point, ordering), 6 weeks live.
- Money: about ₹1.5 to 2 lakh† total, covering intro discounts, free late meals, packaging, delivery, insurance and losses at low volume.

**25. How do we measure success, and when do we kill it?**
Pre-launch smoke test (2 weeks, before any kitchen contract): a pickup-point sign-up page and pre-paid ₹99 first meals.
- **Go:** 150+ pre-paid sign-ups from one park.
- **Kill:** under 50.

Live pilot (6 weeks):

| Metric | Target | Kill line |
|---|---|---|
| Meals per drop (average) | 40+ | under 20 by week 6 |
| Week-6 retention of week-1 eaters | 50%+ | under 40% |
| On-time (at pickup by 12:55) | 95%+ | under 85% |
| "Bad meal" credits | under 5% of meals | over 10% |
| Contribution per meal | positive | negative after week 4 |

Any kill line hit at week 6 means we stop, write down what we learned, and don't open a second park.

**26. If it works, how big can it get?**
Park by park. Bengaluru has a few dozen large tech parks† and ORR, Whitefield and Electronic City are dense with exactly this customer. Each park that reaches ~350 meals/day is a small profitable unit. It is a solid local business, not a venture-scale one, unless the B2B employer channel (Q21) opens up.

## Verdict

**CHANGE IT:** "a tiffin service for office workers in Bengaluru" is a crowded, thin-margin commodity that loses to ₹89 platform meals and subsidised cafeterias, so it only has a shot as a one-tech-park, pay-only-for-office-days lunch with batched drops, proven by 150 pre-paid sign-ups and a full-time ops owner before anyone signs a kitchen contract.
